// WHAT IS ENCRYPTION?

A field guide to keeping secrets in the open

THE CORE IDEA

Encryption is the process of scrambling information so that only someone with the right key can read it. Think of it like a padlock on a message — anyone can see the locked box, but only the person with the key can open it.

Modern encryption uses complex mathematics to convert plain text (readable data) into ciphertext (scrambled data). The transformation is designed to be practically impossible to reverse without the correct key — even with the most powerful computers alive.

On this site, every message you type is encrypted with AES-256 before it travels across the channel. The ciphertext — that wall of hex characters — is what the internet actually sees.

A SHORT HISTORY

~1900 BC
Caesar & Substitution Ciphers
Ancient Egyptians used non-standard hieroglyphs. Julius Caesar shifted letters by a fixed number — "A" becomes "D". Simple, but it worked until someone figured out the pattern.
1467
The Vigenère Cipher
Leon Battista Alberti invented polyalphabetic substitution — multiple Caesar shifts based on a keyword. Called "le chiffre indéchiffrable" for nearly 300 years.
1940s
Enigma & Modern Cryptanalysis
Nazi Germany used the Enigma machine — 158 quintillion possible settings. Alan Turing cracked it at Bletchley Park, shortening WWII and laying the foundations of computer science.
1976
Public-Key Cryptography
Diffie and Hellman introduced key pairs — a public key anyone can use to encrypt, a private key only you can decrypt. This solved the oldest problem in cryptography: sharing a secret in public.
1977
RSA Algorithm
Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman published RSA — based on the near-impossibility of factoring very large numbers. Still in widespread use today.
2001
AES Becomes the Global Standard
AES, developed by Belgian cryptographers Daemen and Rijmen, was adopted by the U.S. government. AES-256 is what protects your banking, messages, and passwords right now.
Today
E2E Encryption & Post-Quantum Cryptography
Apps like Signal use end-to-end encryption so not even the server can read your messages. Researchers now race to build encryption that will survive quantum computers.

HOW AES-256 ACTUALLY WORKS

AES uses a 256-bit key and processes data in 128-bit blocks through 14 rounds of mathematical transformations — substitutions, shifts, mixes, and key additions — until the output looks like pure noise.

PLAINTEXT→hello alice
KEY→3a9f2c1e8b4d7f6a0e5c...
ROUND 1→9d4f2a8c1b7e3d6f0c5a...
ROUND 7→f3c8a1e6b2d9f4c0e7a3...
ROUND 14→a8f3c21b9e4d7a6f2c1e...
CIPHER→a8f3c21b9e4d7a6f2c1e8b5d0f9a3b7c...

Without the key, the ciphertext reveals nothing. Brute-forcing AES-256 would take longer than the age of the universe — even with every computer on Earth.

WHY IT MATTERS

🏦

FINANCIAL SAFETY

Every online payment and bank transfer is encrypted in transit. Without it, your card number is visible to anyone on the network.

🗣️

FREE SPEECH

Journalists and activists in authoritarian countries depend on encrypted communication to speak freely without surveillance.

🏥

MEDICAL PRIVACY

Medical records and test results are legally required to be encrypted. Your health history stays between you and your doctor.

🔐

IDENTITY PROTECTION

Passwords are stored as encrypted hashes. Even if a database is stolen, attackers only get a useless scramble.
